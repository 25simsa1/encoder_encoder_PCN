## 2305.13562  [full]  VERDICT: DISTINGUISH

**Account.** From the full text, Alonso, Krichmar and Neftci establish three things about supervised/autoencoder PCNs (chain MLPs and small convnets, 3-5 hidden layers, batch 64, SVHN/CIFAR-10/Tiny ImageNet). (1) Sequential inference (Sec 5, Figs 3 and 7, Algorithms 2 and 4). The standard simultaneous (Jacobi-style) inference schedule has an error-propagation delay that is intrinsically a chain phenomenon, "the standard implementation of the inference phase requires at least T≥(L−2) in order for non-zero errors to form at each hidden layer"; updating layers one at a time output-to-back (Gauss-Seidel) propagates non-zero error to every layer in one iteration, so T=1-5 suffices (Fig 4 shows IL-Adam degrading sharply at small T while SeqIL-Adam is flat). (2) Theory (Sec 3, Thms 1-2, App A.2/A.4). IL with static per-layer step sizes equals the proximal/implicit-SGD update, but only under specific gamma settings, mini-batch 1, and the limit where the weight step fully annihilates local errors; from that assumption the IL update approximates a Hessian-shrunk gradient −(I+βH)⁻¹β∇L, i.e. damped Newton-Raphson for small β, empirically supported by proximal-objective decrease during inference (Figs 1 and 8). (3) MQ optimizer (Sec 4, Fig 2, Eq 7-8). Vanilla IL's weight updates are "drastically smaller in magnitude in early layers, which may lead the network to get caught in poor local minima near the initial parameters"; MQ equalizes per-matrix update magnitude with one adaptive scalar per matrix at O(M) memory (Table 3) and matches Adam-level accuracy (Table 2). All endpoints are in-distribution test accuracy or reconstruction loss; there is no representation-geometry, transfer, or cross-modal endpoint anywhere, and Sec 8 concedes untested scaling beyond 3-5 hidden layers.

Corollary 4.3 shows that under standard settings (gamma_L = Theta(1)) the preconditioner is O(1/M) and PC's gradient reduces to first-order GD at infinite width; Fig 2 and Sec 4.2 (citing Benzing 2022) show even the GN-like end (gamma_L = Theta(M)) is directionally close to GD at large width. ## 1910.12151  [full]  VERDICT: DISTINGUISH

**Account.** Orchard & Sun (arXiv 1910.12151) study why a discriminatively-trained Whittington-Bogacz PC net, when you clamp the output class (beta=0), unclamp the input (alpha=0), and relax to equilibrium, generates nonsense rather than class-like inputs (Sec 2.1.3, Fig 3). Their diagnosis is an ill-posed inverse: for a linear 2-layer net with r<=n<m, the equilibrium constraint reduces to Mx=Y, which has infinitely many solutions x=xbar+c*xhat for xhat in null(M), "the vast majority of these states correspond to input samples ... that do not resemble inputs from the training set" (Sec 3.1, Fig 5). Their fix is a min-2-norm constraint imposed as a linear decay term on the weights (M, and W at half rate) and on the state nodes x (Sec 3.2), with Theorem 1 proving (linear case) the min-2-norm solution of Mx=Y_i equals the training input X_i. Empirically decay lifts generated-vs-exemplar correlation from 0.204 to 0.995 (linear) and 0.630 to 0.979 (tanh) (Table 1) and yields recognizable MNIST digits (Fig 7). Load-bearing caveats in the Discussion (Sec 5): decay costs 5-10% discriminative accuracy on MNIST; omitting the W-decay term "often caused the learning to become unstable, resulting in runaway weights"; the Whittington-Bogacz accelerated alternating x/epsilon update "could lead to unpredictable results in generative mode"; and "'decay' might need to be interpreted slightly differently when using Adam." The paper is single-modality, in-sample, small-scale, with no held-out generalization test and no representation-geometry analysis.

**Claim 1.** threatens (competing lever, partial): our 8-cell dissociation concludes "only the movement axis flips collapse," but they show degenerate PC generation is an ill-posed-inverse failure fixed by a min-2-norm decay regularizer that REDUCES weight norm rather than by more movement/training ("the generative problem is ill-posed, and there are many network states that are consistent with a desired output class," Sec 5), i.e. a fourth lever (min-norm decay) our {width x anti-mean loss x movement} grid never tested; overlap is only partial because their failure is off-manifold inversion, not collapse-to-the-mean.

**Claim 2.** orthogonal: their evidence is single-modality, in-sample exemplar correlation (Table 1, corr>0.99 with decay) with no held-out or cross-modal generalization test, so it bears on nothing in our chance-level held-out coupling numbers (8k 6/14000 p=0.70; 20k 10/14000 p=0.17).

**Claim 3.** orthogonal: they never compare backprop-through-the-unrolled-relaxation against the local relax-then-step rule, nor test InfoNCE, so they do not touch the rule-clause or the optimizer exoneration; two tangential asides only lightly echo us (their accelerated alternating x/epsilon update "could lead to unpredictable results in generative mode," and "'decay' might need to be interpreted slightly differently when using Adam," both Sec 5), neither threatening a number.

**Claim 4.** threatens (competing mechanism): our Claim 4 says the failure is mean-collapse that is "a property of DESCENT ON F, not of PC" and that there is "no retrieval rescue by centering/Procrustes/whitening/ridge," but they locate degenerate generation in PC inference non-uniqueness and fix it inside PC with a min-2-norm weight+state decay proven to recover training inputs (Theorem 1: "the minimum 2-norm solution x* to Ax=Y_i is x* = X_i"), so a reviewer can argue our collapse is their non-uniqueness and that our ridge/whitening battery should have included training-time min-norm decay; we distinguish because their fix matches in-sample inputs (Theorem 1, Table 1) with no repulsion/uniformity term, which cannot manufacture the InfoNCE-style uniformity our geometry (rank ~1 vs 40-115) shows is required for held-out per-pair discrimination.
